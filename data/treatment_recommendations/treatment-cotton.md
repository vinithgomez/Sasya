# Treatment Recommendations — Cotton (Batch 6 of 6 — FINAL crop group)

## Cotton_Bacterial_Blight
**Causal organism:** *Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. malvacearum* (also cited as *X. campestris pv. malvacearum*)
**Symptoms:** Attacks the plant at every stage from seed to harvest, with five recognized phases:
1. *Seedling blight:* small, water-soaked, circular/irregular lesions on cotyledons, spreading to the stem via the petiole, causing seedling withering and death
2. *Angular leaf spot:* small dark-green water-soaked spots on the leaf underside, becoming angular (restricted by leaf veins) as they enlarge, turning reddish-brown with age, visible on both leaf surfaces
3. *Vein blight/vein necrosis (black vein):* veins and veinlets blacken; bacterial ooze forms as crusts on the leaf underside; affected leaves crinkle, twist inward, and wither; spreads to the petiole, causing defoliation
4. *Black arm:* dark brown-to-black lesions on stems/fruiting branches that can girdle them, causing premature leaf drop, stem cracking, gumming, and stems breaking to hang as dry black twigs (the disease's namesake symptom)
5. *Square rot/boll rot:* water-soaked lesions on bolls turning dark/sunken, spreading through the whole boll, causing premature bursting; lint gets stained yellow by bacterial ooze and loses market value; seeds are also infected, reducing size and viability
**Favourable conditions:** soil temp ~28°C, air temp 30-40°C, relative humidity 85%, early sowing, delayed thinning, poor tillage, late irrigation, potassium-deficient soil; rain followed by bright sunshine in October-November is especially favorable.
**Management:**
- Delint cotton seeds with concentrated sulphuric acid @ 100ml/kg seed
- Treat delinted seeds with carboxin or oxycarboxin @ 2g/kg, OR soak seeds in 1000 ppm Streptomycin sulphate overnight
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris; rogue out volunteer cotton plants and weed hosts
- Crop rotation with non-host crops
- Early thinning, early earthing-up with potash
- Grow resistant varieties: **Sujatha, 1412, CRH 71**
- Spray Streptomycin sulphate + Tetracycline mixture @ 100g, combined with Copper oxychloride @ 1.25 kg/ha
**Source:** eAGRI (Indian agricultural university e-course repository, Plant Pathology course PATH272) — http://eagri.org/eagri50/PATH272/lecture13/008.html

## Cotton_Curl_Virus
**Causal organism:** Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV) — a begomovirus (family *Geminiviridae*), single-stranded circular DNA, bipartite genome
**Symptoms:** Downward and upward curling of leaves, thickening of veins, enations (small outgrowths) on the underside of leaves; in severe infection, all leaves curl and plant growth is retarded; boll-bearing capacity is reduced. **Note:** this is one of the most economically damaging cotton diseases in India and Pakistan — described in broader literature as a major limiting factor for cotton production across the Indian subcontinent, with documented outbreaks causing 37-64% incidence in parts of northwest India (Haryana, Punjab, Rajasthan) in some years.
**Vector/spread:** transmitted by the whitefly *Bemisia tabaci* (viruliferous, i.e. virus-carrying whiteflies); alternate/cultivated host plants serve as year-round virus reservoirs; **not transmitted by seed or contact** — control must target the insect vector, not the plant directly.
**Management:**
- Time planting dates to avoid peak whitefly vector population
- Eliminate volunteer perennial cotton and alternate malvaceous hosts (e.g. wild okra)
- Biological vector control: the fungus *Paecilomyces farinosus* parasitizes whitefly and reduces vector population
- Foliar application of neem leaf extract + 1% neem oil — shown to reduce virus transmission by up to 80% in field studies
- Granular systemic insecticides for direct vector management
**Source:** eAGRI Plant Pathology course — http://eagri.org/eagri50/PATH272/lecture13/009.html, cross-confirmed for Indian-context severity by peer-reviewed research (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7176085/, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7004920/)

## Cotton_Fusarium_Wilt
**Causal organism:** *Fusarium oxysporum f.sp. vasinfectum*
**Symptoms:** Yellowing and browning of cotyledons, followed by browning of the petiole; loss of leaf turgidity, yellowing, drooping, and wilting; browning/blackening of internal vascular tissue (visible as streaks when the stem is cut); infected plants are stunted with fewer bolls.
**Favourable conditions:** soil temperature 20-30°C; hot, dry periods followed by rain; heavy black (alkaline) soils; excess nitrogen/phosphatic fertilizer; wounds from root-knot nematode (*Meloidogyne incognita*) or Ash weevil grubs (*Myllocerus pustulatus*) increase susceptibility by creating entry points.
**Management:**
- Remove and burn infected plant debris after deep summer ploughing
- Apply farmyard manure or other organic manure @ 4 tonnes/acre
- Mixed cropping with non-host plants to reduce soil temperature via shading
- Treat acid-delinted seeds with Chlorothalonil @ 4g/kg seed
- Seed treatment with *Bacillus subtilis* @ 10g/kg, OR *Trichoderma asperellum* @ 4g/kg
- Soil application of *Trichoderma asperellum* @ 1kg/acre, twice — at sowing and again at 90 days after sowing (multiply 1kg of T. asperellum in 100kg farmyard manure for 15 days before application)
- Spot drenching with Carbendazim 50% WP @ 1g/litre water
**Source:** TNAU Agritech Portal — https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/cotton_diseases/cotton_d1.html
