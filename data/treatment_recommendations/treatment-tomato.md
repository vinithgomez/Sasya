# Treatment Recommendations — Tomato (Batch 1 of 6 crop groups)

Sourcing approach: prioritized TNAU (Tamil Nadu Agricultural University) Agritech Portal — an Indian government agricultural university source — since it matches the project's regional-India differentiator. Where TNAU doesn't have a dedicated page for a class, this is flagged explicitly rather than silently filled in from a generic/US source.

**Status: 6 of 10 classes fully sourced from TNAU. 3 classes (Leaf Mold, Spider Mites, Target Spot) NOT YET sourced — see gaps section at the end.**

## Tomato_Bacterial_Spot
**Causal organism:** *Xanthomonas axonopodis pv. vesicatoria*
**Symptoms:** Small, brown, water-soaked, circular leaf spots with yellow halo; defoliation of older leaves; small water-soaked spots on green fruit that become irregular, light brown, sunken and scabby.
**Management:**
- Use disease-free seed
- Spray Streptocycline 40-100 ppm (20 g/acre)
**Source:** TNAU Agritech Portal — https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_11.html

## Tomato_Early_Blight
**Causal organism:** *Alternaria solani*
**Symptoms:** Brown spots with concentric rings in a bullseye pattern, yellow margin; fruit infected through calyx/stem attachment with brown concentric rings.
**Management:**
- Remove and destroy crop debris; crop rotation
- Seed treatment: *Trichoderma asperellum* @ 4g/kg seed
- Spray one of: Iprodione 50% WP @ 600 g/ac, Azoxystrobin 23% SC @ 200 ml/ac, Mancozeb 35% SC @ 1 kg/ac, or several other listed fungicide options (see source for full list)
**Source:** TNAU Agritech Portal — https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_2.html

## Tomato_Late_Blight
**Causal organism:** *Phytophthora infestans*
**Symptoms:** Water-soaked black lesions on leaves/stems, rapidly expanding to necrosis; white sporulation on leaves; dark brown lesions and soft rot on fruit.
**Management:**
- Ensure proper drainage; crop rotation
- Soil application of *Trichoderma asperellum (viride)* @ 1.0 kg/acre
- Spray one of: Cyazofamid 34.5% SC @ 80 ml/ac, Azoxystrobin 23% SC @ 200 ml/ac, Mancozeb 35% SC @ 1000 g/ac (see source for full list)
**Source:** TNAU Agritech Portal — https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_8.html

## Tomato_Septoria_Leaf_Spot
**Causal organism:** *Septoria lycopersici*
**Symptoms:** Small round-to-irregular spots with grey centre and dark margin; spots coalesce causing blight and complete defoliation; stems and flowers also affected.
**Management:**
- Remove and destroy affected plant parts
- Seed treatment: *Trichoderma asperellum* @ 4g/kg seed
- Spray Fluxapyroxad 250 g/l + Pyraclostrobin 250 g/l SC @ 200-250 ml/ha
**Source:** TNAU Agritech Portal — https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_4.html

## Tomato_Yellow_Leaf_Curl_Virus
**Causal organism:** Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (TLCV), vectored by whiteflies (*Bemisia tabaci*)
**Symptoms:** Severe stunting; downward rolling/crinkling of leaves; older leaves leathery and brittle; shortened internodes; bushy appearance from excess lateral branching.
**Management:**
- Raise nursery under protected conditions
- Grow sorghum, maize, or bajra as a barrier crop
- Use disease-free seedlings
- Yellow sticky traps @ 5/acre
- Remove alternate weed host (*Abutilon indicum*)
- Spray one of: Dimethoate 30 EC @ 200 ml/ac, Thiamethoxam 25 WG @ 200 g/ac, Imidacloprid 17.8 SL @ 200 ml/ac (targets the whitefly vector, not the virus itself — there is no direct antiviral treatment)
**Source:** TNAU Agritech Portal — https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_6.html
**Note for app copy:** should clarify to the user that this treats the whitefly vector, since the virus itself is not curable once infected — consistent with what global sources (Agrio, Farm Progress) also confirm: no direct antiviral treatment exists.

## Tomato_Mosaic_Virus
**Causal organism:** Cucumber Mosaic Virus (labeled by TNAU as "Tomato Mosaic Virus" causal organism, some naming overlap in literature between ToMV and CMV — worth a note, not fully disambiguated here)
**Symptoms:** Light/dark green mottling on leaves; wilting of young leaves; leaflet distortion and puckering; "fern leaf" and "shoestring" symptoms; stunted growth.
**Management:**
- Use disease-free seeds
- Remove infected plants
- Crop rotation
**Source:** TNAU Agritech Portal — https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato_diseases_12.html

## Tomato_Spider_Mites
**Causal organism:** Red spider mite, *Tetranychus spp.* (a mite pest, not a fungal/bacterial disease — this is why it wasn't on TNAU's disease page; it's catalogued under TNAU's separate Insect/Pest pages instead)
**Symptoms:** Affected leaves turn reddish-brown and bronzy; severe infestation produces silken webbing on leaves, which wither and dry; flower and fruit formation affected.
**Management:**
- Spray wettable sulphur 50 WP @ 2 g/litre, or dicofol 18.5 EC @ 2.5 ml/litre
**Source:** TNAU Agritech Portal (Insect Pest section) — https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/tomato/tomato_7.html

## Tomato_Leaf_Mold
**Causal organism:** *Passalora fulva* (formerly *Fulvia fulva* / *Cladosporium fulvum*)
**Symptoms:** Pale green/yellow spots without well-defined margins on upper leaf surface, turning distinctly yellow; olive-green velvety mold visible on the lower leaf surface; lesions coalesce in severe cases, foliage dies; primarily a disease of high-humidity greenhouse-grown tomatoes, less common in open field.
**Management:**
- Improve ventilation/air circulation — stake and prune plants, space them further apart
- Avoid wetting leaves when watering
- Remove crop residue/debris from the field; rotate with non-tomato vegetables
- Preventative fungicide program: chlorothalonil, mancozeb, or copper-based fungicide
- Grow resistant cultivars where available
**Source:** consensus across multiple university extension sources — Clemson HGIC (https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/tomato-diseases-disorders/), UMass Amherst CAFE (https://ag.umass.edu/vegetable/fact-sheets/tomato-leaf-mold), University of Kentucky (https://veggiescout.mgcafe.uky.edu/node/154). **Not TNAU-sourced** — flagged since this is a greenhouse-associated disease more documented in temperate-climate extension literature than in Indian sources found so far.

## Tomato_Target_Spot
**Causal organism:** *Corynespora cassiicola*
**Symptoms:** Starts on older leaves, spreading upward; small necrotic lesions with light-brown centers and dark margins on leaves, sometimes with a ring/target pattern; on fruit, brown slightly sunken flecks that develop into larger pitted areas; spreads rapidly in wet weather and can defoliate/destroy the whole plant. Important disease in tropical/subtropical regions — relevant to Indian growing conditions even though the sourced literature below is primarily Florida/UF-IFAS-based.
**Management:**
- Cultural: check seedlings in nursery before transplanting; avoid planting new crops next to old infected ones; remove lower leaves after fruit trusses are picked; weed control
- Chemical: fungicides proven effective include chlorothalonil, azoxystrobin, pyraclostrobin, boscalid, and a famoxadone+mancozeb combination product
- Resistance management: rotate fungicide classes — strobilurin-resistant strains of *C. cassiicola* have been documented, so repeated use of the same fungicide class risks resistance development
**Source:** University of Florida IFAS Extension (https://ipm.ifas.ufl.edu/pdfs/Target_Spot.pdf, https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/PP351). **Not TNAU-sourced** — flagged since this came from US/tropical-region extension literature rather than an Indian source, though the disease itself is explicitly noted as a tropical/subtropical concern, so the biology and chemical recommendations should transfer reasonably well to Indian growing conditions.

No treatment needed — app should return a positive/no-action message when this class is predicted.

## Summary — sourcing transparency

7 of 10 classes sourced directly from TNAU (Indian government agricultural university): Bacterial Spot, Early Blight, Late Blight, Septoria Leaf Spot, Yellow Leaf Curl Virus, Mosaic Virus, Spider Mites.

2 of 10 classes (Leaf Mold, Target Spot) sourced from international/US university extension services, clearly flagged as such — TNAU did not have dedicated coverage for these two, likely because Leaf Mold is more of a greenhouse/temperate-climate concern and Target Spot literature is more developed in Florida/tropical-America sources, even though the disease itself is relevant to Indian tropical conditions.

1 of 10 (Healthy) needs no sourcing.
